# Math Ninja: math goals

What the game is trying to teach, how good math practice works, what's done, and ideas for later.
Players: young kids, kindergarten to about 3rd grade.

## What good practice looks like

Research on learning math facts agrees on a few things:

1. **Tricks before speed.** Kids go from counting, to tricks (make 10, doubles, ×9 = ×10 − 1), to just knowing the answer. Drilling speed too early mostly trains fast counting.
2. **Recall practice.** Pulling the answer out of memory works better than spotting it in a list of choices. Typing or saying it is stronger than picking it.
3. **Spacing.** Coming back to a fact on later days works better than lots of repeats in one sitting.
4. **Mixing.** After a skill is learned, mix it with older ones.
5. **Show the right answer right after a miss.** This is one of the strongest effects.
6. **Short, frequent sessions.** 5–10 focused minutes a day. Keep time pressure low-stakes: timed tests cause anxiety, gentle speed bonuses are fine.
7. **Fluency means about 3 seconds or less per fact.**

## What the game already does

- Silver (fast once) and gold (fast again on another day) facts; the Daily Dojo brings back missed and due facts first.
- Missed facts come back later in the same level ("You remembered it!"); about 1 question in 5 is a review.
- Sensei teaches tricks (make 10, make the next ten: 47 + 3 = 50 then 2 more, near doubles, ×9, think in tens) with pictures (arrays, number lines, groups).
- Think skills: missing numbers and balance problems teach what "=" really means.
- Wrong choices are real kid mistakes (off by one or one step, swapped digits, wrong operation), so guessing doesn't pay.
- Unlocks come from mastery, not time spent. 7 × 5 and 5 × 7 count as one fact.
- A miss costs no heart, so mistakes are low-stakes.

## Done in this round

- **A miss always shows the right answer** ("Oops! 7 × 8 = 56") at gates and in boss fights. Animals, ? boxes and stepping stones already did.
- **Tighter "fast" for times and divide:** 3 seconds with buttons (6 when Kai walks or bonks). Adding and taking away keep 5 seconds (10). The Daily Dojo uses the same limits.
- **New math needs facts that stuck:** auto mode moves on when 90% of facts are learned **and** half are gold (remembered on two different days). In between, the end-of-level card says "🥇 4/9 · Come back tomorrow!"
- **Grade tracks (mixing + challenge):** with a grade set, each world favours one kind of math (Japan add, Lava take away, Robot times, Snow divide, Castle think): level 1 is only that kind, levels 2–3 and the boss mix about 40% of it with the grade's other kinds (interleaving). Each kind is judged on its own after a level: a great result steps its next level 1 up one skill, a hard one steps it back (Sensei helps after one miss). The next grade starts when every kind is passed. Reviews are 1 in 4.
- **Training rooms can't be mashed:** taps in the first 0.4 s don't count, a miss knocks the bar back and pauses Kai (longer for misses in a row), and steps need at most 1 miss in the last 8. Simulated: random tapping finishes within 10 minutes about 2% of the time; a kid at 90% is as fast as before.

- **Typed answers in the Daily Dojo:** facts already known (silver or gold) are typed on a keypad (recall, not recognition); new and practice facts are still picked. "One more round" button.
- **Slow-but-right facts:** right but slow twice in a row puts a fact on the practice list (levels' reviews and the Dojo) without changing its colour; a fast answer clears it.
- **Races use the real answer time:** fast = steered into the right lane soon after the gate came up (no free silver).
- **More mixing after mastery:** done through the grade tracks (world mixes, reviews 1 in 4 from every learned skill).

## Ideas for later (not chosen yet)

Most useful first:

1. **More typing (recall).** The Daily Dojo types known facts now. Next options: gates show a keypad in expert mode or for gold facts; a "say it first" parent tip in the README.
2. **A stronger Daily Dojo, the rest:** a small streak reward kids can see grow (pet, belt stripe, Dojo wall); 15–20 questions.
3. **Show the trick when it's needed, not only after 2 misses.** For example, the first time a new fact type appears (first make-10 fact, first ×9), Sensei shows the trick before the question.
4. **Kindergarten number sense** (before adding):
   - Counting objects, "how many?" with dots or ten-frames instead of written numbers.
   - Which is more, comparing numbers.
   - Number bonds with pictures (5 = 2 + 3 shown as dots).
   - Fits the "gem grids" idea (arrays: "how many?") already on the ideas list.
5. **3rd grade topics beyond facts** (big parts of the 3rd grade standards):
   - **Fractions** (the biggest gap): halves, thirds, fourths of a shape or bar; fractions on a number line; which is bigger.
   - Rounding to the nearest 10 / 100.
   - Area as rows × columns (ties into arrays).
   - Telling time, money.
6. **Short word problems.** *(Started: the hungry animal tells its problem as a one-line story for 2nd grade and up.)* One-line stories that fit the world ("Kai has 3 bags of 4 fish. How many fish?") so kids practice choosing the operation, not just doing it.
7. **Parent view.** One screen per kid: facts still red, slow facts, minutes and questions per day, so you know what to practice off-screen.

## How to use it at home

- A Daily Dojo every day, plus a level or two, works better than one long session a week.
- Have the kids say the answer out loud before tapping: that turns a pick into real recall.
- Red facts in the Fact Book are great for 2-minute car or dinner quizzes.
